The headline, without the technical ledger

What we found

Herbert Simon’s 1955 paper fits the Yule distribution to word counts and to counts of authors by number of papers, using one stated recipe, and calls the fit “reasonably good”. Recomputed, no convention he states reproduces any whole column except Chicago’s, and his own reading of where the fit fails partly reverses under his recipe. On the source tables, the one-parameter Yule refitted by likelihood is rejected by a pre-registered test for three of his four author tables (an iid parametric bootstrap of the refitted model, not a test of his printed estimates). Power laws with an exponential cutoff fit three of the four better, and Simon’s own two-parameter generalization is the only model not rejected on Eldridge’s word count.

What is new here

Under his own stated recipe, Simon’s sentence that the estimates are “too high for the tails” in three of four cases becomes one of four. The printed f(2) cells run low, and since every printed “11 or more” cell is the remainder after rows 1–10, the shortfall lands in the tail by construction. No stated convention reproduces any whole column except Chicago’s (of 77 estimate cells, 62 are exact or reproduce under a stated convention, 4 are rounding slips, 11 reproduce under none), and the 1957 reprint in Models of Man prints all 152 table cells unchanged, slips included. These are reproducibility findings about a 1955 hand computation, with no motive inferred; a cross-family audit re-derived the tail arithmetic from the page.

Moments against likelihood

Simon’s recipe, α = n/k, is the method-of-moments estimate of the Yule ρ; the likelihood estimate sits below it on five of six data sets. Simulated at the real sample sizes, his constant-rate process (§II) did not produce that gap in 1,000 runs for any set. The declining rate of Simon and Van Wormer (1963) is consistent with the gap and the head shape for three of the four author sets, at one or two adjacent points of a fixed grid, and none of the six settings matches Chem. This stage had a same-family code audit only.

The fit and its rivals

Sichel’s 1985 parameter for the chemistry authors, as reported by Bogachev et al. (2023), is reproduced to its five reported decimals (θ = 0.968759). The extended negative binomial beats the Yule on Chem by ΔAIC 66.6, failing a prediction made before the fit; for Chem the cutoff result agrees with Sichel (as reported) and Smolinsky (2015), and the comparison on the other author tables is new. For Physicists the Yule is not rejected by the 11-cell test (p = 0.064, 32 of 500 bootstrap replicates; simulation interval 0.044–0.089), which does not show that it fits well: the supplementary KS test rejects it.

Word counts

On three recounts of Ulysses from Project Gutenberg — not a replication of Hanley’s 1937 index — Simon’s equation (4.2) is not rejected by the 11-cell test. Only the product cβ is identified: 0.145–0.162, compatible with his c ≈ 0.2 for suitable β. For Eldridge, cβ is about 0.02 but (4.2) is rejected. “ρ about one” holds on a binned log-log slope; by likelihood only narrowly for the recounts, and not for Eldridge.

What the headline does not mean

  • The goodness-of-fit test is an iid parametric bootstrap of a newly fitted Yule, not a test of Simon’s printed estimates or of his dependent growth process, and not the only reading of “reasonably good”.
  • Not rejecting a model is not evidence that it fits; 0 of 500 exceedances bounds the bootstrap probability only below about 0.006.
  • Most checks were fresh-context reviews by the same model family. One cross-family audit (Codex) re-derived the Stage 1 tail arithmetic from the page, the Stage 4 grade table from all 20,000 saved bootstrap replicates, the P7a and P20 fits and the 198 plotted values; it did not regenerate the bootstrap, certify every rival’s optimizer, or check the Stage 3 simulations, which had a same-family code audit only. Shared blind spots of one family are not ruled out.
  • The pre-registration’s frozen versions are checkable by anyone; the freeze times are attested only by the implementing session’s own log.

The question

Simon's recipe α = n/k is the method-of-moments (MoM) estimate of the Yule ρ. On the real tables it sits well above the likelihood estimate. The gap is G = ρ_MLE(censored) − ρ_MoM.

Is a gap this size what Simon's processes produce at these sample sizes, and if so, which process?

Results

The estimators are sound. Profile-likelihood 95% intervals on iid Yule samples cover the planted ρ in 93.5–95.3% of 1,000 replicates per set. That is inside the frozen band in every set.

§II, constant α (the recipe's own premise), produces no gap.

  • Its median G is −0.023 to 0.000 in every set.
  • Each observed G lies outside its 95% interval: at the 0.0th percentile for the five negative gaps, and at the 99.8th for Ulysses, whose gap has the opposite sign.
  • So the distance between moments and likelihood is not estimator noise. At these sample sizes it is evidence against the constant-α process itself.

Case II, declining α, produces the gap's direction.

  • Its median G falls monotonically as the decline steepens (β = 0.9 → 0.6), in every set.
  • Joint reproduction (G, p1 = f(1)/n and r21 = f(2)/f(1) all inside the null's 95% interval) occurs for Chicago at β = 0.7 and 0.6, and for Physicists and Econometrica at β = 0.9.

§III, the slice with whole-type deletion, is much more variable.

  • Its 95% interval for n/k is 2.8–19× wider than §II's.
  • It reproduces Econometrica and Eldridge jointly.

**Nothing in the frozen grid reproduces Chem or Ulysses jointly.**

  • Chem: its gap lies between Case II β = 0.9 (median −0.137) and 0.8 (median −0.258). But f(1)/n and f(2)/f(1) both fall outside the null at both neighbours, and f(2)/f(1) also falls outside under §II and §III. So a β between the grid points would fix G but not the shape.
  • ***Ulysses*:** §III covers its G but not its head shape.

Reading

  • Simon & Van Wormer's direction holds. Moments run above likelihood when α declines, as they said. In this grid, declining α is consistent with the gap and the head shape together for three of the four bibliometric sets, at one or two adjacent grid points (Chicago at β 0.7 and 0.6; Physicists and Econometrica at 0.9). Econometrica is also reproduced by §III, so the grid does not single out one mechanism there.
  • No single mechanism covers all six sets. The largest bibliometric set (Chem, n = 6,891) is reproduced by none of the six process settings. That is consistent with Simon's own closing remark on Table 3 that "a further refinement of the model is apparently needed".
  • ***Ulysses* behaves differently.** Its likelihood estimate is above MoM. Its data are thinner, though (f(1..3) only, censored at 4).

Caveats

  • β is a grid, not a fit. "Reproduces" means each of three statistics lies inside its own 95% interval. It is not a joint test. With up to six settings per set, some coverage by chance is expected. Treat a single ✓ as "consistent with", not as identification.
  • Case II's n runs slightly short. Clipping α(t) at 1 makes the expected n fall short of the target at low β: up to 4.9% (Econometrica, β = 0.6). Every joint reproduction occurs where the shortfall is ≤ 2.4%. Each replicate's MoM uses its own n and k, so G is internally consistent.
  • §III snapshots can be shorter than k. The median snapshot length is within 0.4% of k in every set. The 2.5th percentile is lower, down to 64% of k for Chicago, because deleting one large type removes many tokens at once. This is the literal Amendment 2 rule.
  • The §III convergence check passed only in an approximate sense. For Physicists, the medians of p1 and G at B = 3k and 6k differ by less than one null-SD. Here the null-SD is approximated as the 95% width / 3.92.
  • Data limits. Econometrica rests on Simon's printed table only. Ulysses rests on f(1..3), n and k, censored at 4, so it is not comparable with the others' censoring at 11.

Models. Eight, all on x ≥ 1, fitted by maximum likelihood:

  • Yule;
  • zeta;
  • log-series;
  • Sichel's GIGP with ν = −0.5 and α = 0, which is an extended negative binomial (ENB) with r = −0.5;
  • Simon's own Waring generalization (3.8);
  • the Yule with Simon's convergence factor b^i (1.1);
  • the ENB/ZTNB with r free;
  • the full three-parameter GIGP.

Data.

  • Full tables for Chicago, Chem, Physicists and Eldridge.
  • Econometrica only as Simon printed it: rows 1–10 plus 11+.

Gates. Before any data fit, each pmf passed three checks:

  • it normalizes to 1 within 10⁻⁶;
  • it agrees with an independent mixture sampler, for every family that has one;
  • the nesting identities hold (Waring c = 0, cutoff b = 1, GIGP α → 0 = ENB).

The zeta fit also reproduces Smolinsky's Chem MLE of 1.9665.

An exploratory X² (tail pooled to expected ≥ 5; 32 cells, 30 df) gives p = 0.88, against his 91.8% (also as reported by Bogachev et al.) on a grouping that is not stated. "Reproduces exactly" means the MLE rounds to the reported five decimals.

It is not a test of Simon's printed estimates, nor a calibrated test of his dependent growth process (Stage 3 asks that), nor the only possible reading of his qualitative phrase.

Why P7a failed. It came from arithmetic done before the freeze (a lead recorded in Amendment 3):

  • that arithmetic assumed θ ≈ 1;
  • on that assumption the head implies r ≈ −0.47 while a power tail needs r ≈ −0.97;
